## Overview

Fieldnote is a note-capture and synthesis tool built for anthropologists and ethnographers doing long-term fieldwork. Drawing on my own background in anthropology, I designed a system for capturing observations the way researchers actually think — nonlinear, sensory, and layered — rather than forcing them into rigid spreadsheets.

## The problem

Existing research tools were built for surveys and interviews, not embodied fieldwork. Researchers were losing the texture of their observations — smells, gestures, half-formed hunches — by the time they sat down to write them up.

## Process

<ProcessStep number="01" title="Shadowing researchers in the field">
I spent time with three research teams during active fieldwork to understand how notes were actually taken: voice memos, sketches, photos, and fragments jotted on paper.
</ProcessStep>

<ProcessStep number="02" title="Designing for capture speed, not structure">
The first version prioritized structured tagging. It failed. I redesigned around fast, low-friction capture first, with structure and connections added later during synthesis.
</ProcessStep>

<ProcessStep number="03" title="Building a synthesis view">
The hardest part of fieldwork is finding patterns across weeks of notes. I designed a visual "constellation" view that lets researchers cluster observations by theme, place, or person.
</ProcessStep>

## Outcome

Fieldnote is now used by two university research labs for ongoing studies. Researchers reported spending 40% less time on write-ups, since synthesis happened continuously rather than all at once at the end.

## Reflection

Designing for a discipline I trained in myself was a reminder that domain expertise is a design tool — it let me challenge assumptions baked into "standard" research software instead of just polishing them.
