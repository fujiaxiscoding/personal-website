## Overview

Kinfolk helps distributed teams build genuine relationships, not just coordinate work. I designed the core rituals and interaction model for a product meant to feel more like a shared living room than a workplace tool.

## The problem

Remote teams had plenty of tools for meetings and tasks, but few for the informal relationship-building that happens naturally in an office. Leadership teams wanted connection, but most "culture" tools felt performative and were abandoned within weeks.

## Process

<ProcessStep number="01" title="Studying what makes connection feel real">
I interviewed remote employees about the moments that actually made them feel close to coworkers — almost none of them happened in a dedicated "culture" tool.
</ProcessStep>

<ProcessStep number="02" title="Designing lightweight, opt-in rituals">
Rather than mandatory activities, I designed small optional rituals — a weekly reflection prompt, a rotating "get to know you" pairing — that people could engage with on their own terms.
</ProcessStep>

<ProcessStep number="03" title="Testing for authenticity, not just usability">
Standard usability testing didn't capture whether something felt genuine. I ran longitudinal pilots with three teams over four weeks to see what actually got used after the novelty wore off.
</ProcessStep>

## Outcome

Pilot teams saw sustained engagement with weekly rituals after the four-week mark, when most culture tools see steep drop-off. One team lead described it as "the only tool that made remote work feel less lonely."

## Reflection

This project reinforced that designing for human connection requires resisting the urge to over-engineer. The best moments in the product were the quietest — a short prompt, a small nudge, then getting out of the way.
